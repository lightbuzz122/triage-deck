A whole shift's thinking in fifteen minutes.

  1. 1

    Meet the patient

    Talk to them, or to a relative on the phone when they can't give a history. Confused patients behave like it.

  2. 2

    Examine properly

    Move in close. Inspect, palpate, percuss and auscultate. Tap reflexes with the hammer, check pupils with the pen torch.

  3. 3

    Investigate on a budget

    Monitoring, bloods and imaging cost time and tokens. Nothing is ever blocked; the budget teaches stewardship.

  4. 4

    Score, debrief, climb

    The patient improves or deteriorates with what you do. Get scored on critical actions, then post your best to the leaderboard.

Good to know.

Is it really free?

Yes. The sample cases are free to play as often as you like. A monthly plan for the full library will come later, and the free cases will stay free.

How does scoring work?

Each case scores the critical actions and their timing out of 100. Resource use is shown separately as stewardship feedback. Your best score per case counts on the leaderboard.

Is this clinical guidance?

No. Triage Deck is an educational simulation. Cases reference published guidance where it helps learning, but always follow your local protocols and senior advice in real practice.

Which countries is it built for?

Cases are written around practice in the UK, Ireland and Bulgaria. Clinical reasoning transfers well, and where guidance differs the debrief says so.
